## 0. Executive decision

**Public App Store name:** `Lineman Pay` · **Subtitle:** `Verify Pay & Track Hours`<br>
**Primary screenshot headline:** **Is Your Paycheck Correct?**<br>
**Core promise:** **Track what you worked. Compare expected pay with your paystub. Understand possible differences.**
**Category hypothesis:** Finance (confirm in App Store Connect against product fit; not a ranking guarantee).  
**Market:** US employees paid hourly, **starting with workers with variable schedules, overtime, or premiums**, while preserving the current lineman/lineworker capabilities.

**Strategic shift:** From a lineman-specific pay tracker to a **private, evidence-backed paycheck checker and reconciliation tool** for people with variable pay. The lineman product becomes an advanced use-case/profile, **not** discarded code, users, or expertise.

Do **not** expand the promise to a universal tax calculator, employer portal, legal compliance auditor, automatic wage-theft detector, benefits verifier, or guaranteed money recovery. The current engine verifies **expected earnings under confirmed, supported rules** and contrasts them with **user-confirmed paystub fields**. The result may be *match within compared scope*, *possible difference*, or *unable to verify*.

### What the data establishes

1. A broad `paycheck` bid can buy volume, much of which may be **payroll-provider navigational traffic**.
2. Audit/checking terms deserve a controlled test because they match the product's *job-to-be-done*, **not because their market volume is established**.
3. The specialty niche may convert exceptionally well; **do not delete it**.
4. **Unknown:** organic popularity/difficulty, exact-query search volume, install-to-first-audit, first-audit-to-paid, repeat-payday retention, and contribution margin by query cohort.

Avoid claiming “low competition,” “strong ASO ranking,” or “profitable CAC” without independent evidence.

## 2. Target user and positioning

### Job-to-be-done

> “I worked certain hours at particular rates. Before I trust my paycheck, I want to understand what I should have earned, what the stub shows, and why any differences might exist.”

**Primary ICP:** US hourly workers whose overtime, shift patterns, multiple rates, or premiums make payroll verification nontrivial, **who have access to their own hours and paystub**. Start with construction/trades, field service, utility workers, and other variable-schedule employees. Do not imply jurisdiction-specific coverage for an occupation until verified.

**Secondary ICP:** Lineworkers/linemen; high-rules-complexity workers who benefit from existing callout, storm, per-diem, and effective-dated agreement capabilities. Market as **advanced**, not “legacy.”

**Exclude from initial generic promise:** salaried employees wanting net-tax estimates; workers seeking ADP/Paycor/Paychex/Proliant sign-in, direct deposit, or paystub retrieval; employers seeking payroll processing; workers requiring unmodeled bonuses, commission, garnishments, comprehensive tax calculations, or universal employment-law compliance.

### Positioning architecture

- **Category:** Paycheck audit / paycheck checker / paystub comparison.
- **Outcome:** More confidence about what a paycheck does—and does not—match.
- **Practical mechanism:** Confirmed rates + actual work record + confirmed paystub lines → traceable difference.
- **Trust proof:** On-device processing, rule provenance, exact-money arithmetic, unsupported-scope warnings, editable OCR suggestions.
- **Emotional benefit:** Replace uncertainty with an explainable check; **not** guaranteed financial recovery.
- **Anti-positioning:** Not another employer payroll portal, generic time clock, AI legal adjudicator, or bank account.

**One-line pitch:** *A private paycheck checker for hourly workers: track hours, scan a paystub, and spot possible differences.*

**Expanded pitch:** *Lineman Pay helps you estimate pay from the work and rates you confirm, compare it to the numbers on your paystub, and retain the evidence behind possible differences—all without an employer login.*

## 5. Broader product experience: Checker → Audit → Repeat

### User journey A — Quick Paycheck Checker (new, simplest)

**Who:** Hourly worker who knows their hourly rate and hours but not complex agreement rules.

1. Choose **“Check my paycheck”** as the first action. No lineworker terminology, account, or employer system login.
2. Enter pay period, base hourly rate and actual hours. Add OT **only under an explicitly selected and confirmed rule/assumption**.
3. Show **Estimated gross earnings (within configured scope)**. If the period or OT rule is uncertain, show a review state rather than a precise-looking authoritative answer.
4. Import/scan a paystub **or enter stub fields manually**. Confirm OCR fields and pay-period alignment.
5. Compare **like-for-like amounts** (gross to gross or supported wage lines to wage lines). If taxes/deductions or other components are absent, explicitly mark the comparison incomplete.
6. Show either **No difference within compared items**, **Possible difference**, or **Unable to verify**; drill into cause and evidence.
7. Let the user save/export the first complete audit free, consistent with the canonical pricing/entitlement contract.

**Target first value:** estimated earnings in ~90 seconds for a simple, already-informed hourly worker (*design target to test, not observed performance*). Reliable audit completion is higher priority than achieving a speed target through assumed rules.

### User journey B — Recurring Paycheck Audit (retention loop)

**Payday reminder/entry (opt-in) → latest work record → same-period paystub → confirmed comparison → explained difference → export/ask employer → next payday.**

- Support weekly and biweekly cycles first; handle other configured cycles only where already verified.
- Make pay-period overlap/mismatch a prominent blocker, not a silent date guess.
- Reuse confirmed rates/rule versions without applying new rules retroactively.
- Make a “looks consistent within checked fields” outcome just as useful as a discrepancy.
- Keep evidence, history and resolution status local.
- **No invented employer response or automatic recovery.**

### User journey C — Advanced Paycheck Audit for Lineworkers

- Opt into advanced rules/profiles rather than imposing them on everyone.
- Preserve callout, per diem, storm/shift patterns where actually supported, agreement effective dates and evidence chains.
- Maintain specialized landing pages and search campaigns for `lineworker`, `lineman pay`, `callout tracker`.
- Never silently reinterpret stored lineworker history after a generic onboarding redesign.

### Product semantics that may not be collapsed

| Label | Allowed meaning |
|---|---|
| **Estimate** | Computed expected amount under selected, confirmed assumptions |
| **Check / compare** | Side-by-side supported monetary/period fields |
| **Audit** | Traceable comparison with applicable rule coverage and evidence |
| **Possible difference** | A numerical mismatch requiring explanation, not proof employer underpaid |
| **Unable to verify** | Missing/incompatible inputs, unmodeled compensation, unclear OT/jurisdiction, or unmatched period |
| **Verified** | Prefer “input confirmed” / “compared” rather than implying legal or employer certification |

**Hard rule:** A paystub alone usually cannot establish whether all worked hours or premiums were paid; the app must obtain independent work facts and applicable rules, or narrow its claim to arithmetic/document checking.

### Success metrics and decisions

| Stage | Measurement | Initial **experiment gate** (assumption, not benchmark) |
|---|---|---|
| Acquire | Cost per install **and** branded-query contamination | Review any cohort where >20% of spend is clearly irrelevant navigation |
| Activate | Install → configured profile → credible expected pay | Aim ≥50% for qualified generic users; investigate failure modes |
| Audit | Eligible user → completed comparable first audit | Aim ≥30%; separate “not comparable” from abandonment |
| Retain | First → second payday check | Aim ≥40–50% of users with a second payday reached |
| Monetize | Install → paid, first-year receipt / acquisition cost | Don't scale on CPI alone; require credible contribution CAC payback |
| Trust | Material OCR edit/mismatch, incorrect audit, support complaints | **Zero tolerated known unqualified legal-verdict claims**; regression fixtures for every monetary defect |

Sample-size/stop rule: **Do not declare a winning campaign from 1–3 installs.** Prefer at least ~30 comparable audits **per materially tested segment** to identify gross operational failures, and substantially more paid conversions for economic inference. Use uncertainty ranges instead of significance theater. If cohorts remain sparse, run observed usability sessions and retain both names as hypotheses.

**Go broader:** Generic hourly users reach comparable audit and second-payday value without rule ambiguity, AND contribution-adjusted acquisition economics are at least competitive with the specialist segment.  
**Hold specialist-first:** Generic installs fail to audit, mainly want employer portals, or support/coverage costs overwhelm paid conversions.  
**Stop a claim/feature:** Any repeated false certainty about wage entitlement or silent assumption of rules triggers rollback/copy correction irrespective of growth.

## 8. Seven-day execution sequence (smallest evidence-first release)

1. **Day 1:** Save both Apple Ads exports with report period/country/filter settings in an approved non-sensitive research location; map vendor-query contamination and classify visible actual terms. Do not label suppressed terms as exact searches.
2. **Day 2:** Prototype a generic “Check my paycheck” onboarding with manual paystub entry on existing deterministic engine; no backend.
3. **Day 3:** Test with 5 generic hourly workers and 5 lineworkers using **their own data privately on-device** or synthetic fixtures; observe setup time, rules confusion, unsupported cases, and comparison completion. Do not store real employee pay data in repo.
4. **Day 4:** Resolve top three blockers; add explicit period mismatch and coverage states and independent edge-case fixtures.
5. **Day 5:** Produce six draft screenshot compositions from functioning synthetic UI; design review under the canonical visual system.
6. **Day 6:** Start small, separated Apple Ads exact-match experiments with navigational negatives; log funnel metrics consistent with privacy review.
7. **Day 7:** Review **audit completion + second-payday intent + paid evidence**, not raw CPI. Decide whether to submit the broad title this cycle, continue specialist-first, or iterate on activation.

**Work sequencing:** Rename product-facing surfaces **only when** first-audit flow and App Store claims are ready. Actual metadata update, publication, and release are separate authorized operations.

## 9. Strongest counterargument and failure modes

**Counterargument:** Workers searching for “paycheck” frequently want their employer's paystub portal or quick take-home pay, neither of which this app provides. Generic hourly employees may have simple payroll and little motivation to record hours, while linemen have higher-value complex agreements and repeat audit needs. Broadening can increase downloads but *decrease* paid conversion and weaken the brand.

This is plausible and partly supported by the vendor-brand search-term leakage. The solution is **a broader entry experience with a retained narrow capability advantage**, plus a hard paid/retention gate—not a rewrite into a generic paycheck app.

| Failure mode | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Employer-login confusion | “Where is ADP login?” reviews or rapid uninstalls | Clear “not your employer payroll portal” listing and negatives |
| Unsupported legal rules | Many “not comparable” cases / contested outcomes | Narrow market/claims, explicit scope, expand verified fixtures |
| OCR overconfidence | High correction rate; mismatched periods | User-confirmed fields, visible source, manual fallback |
| Setup friction | No first expected-pay result | Quick profile; progressive rules; never default unverified OT |
| Misleading screenshots | High installs, low first-audit completion | Match UI/content and measurable user intent |
| Generic commodities undercut | Users only use free calculator | Paid differentiation around audit evidence/repeat workflow |
| Privacy erosion | Attribution SDK requests payroll content | Reject telemetry; aggregate privacy-safe events or accept measurement limits |
| Existing users disrupted | Lost historical rules, broken entitlement, confusing label | No technical-ID changes, migration and upgrade QA |
