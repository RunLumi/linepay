# Primary sources and rule approval

[Payroll home](README.md) · [Legal baseline](us-legal-baseline.md) · [Coverage](coverage-and-gaps.md)

**Research retrieval date: September 6, 2026.** This is a bounded engineering research register, not counsel approval, a 50-state survey, or confirmation that every supplement to an agreement has been located. Recheck the operative version for the date and place of work before implementing or releasing a legal rule. DOL fact sheets explain rules; the underlying statutes, regulations, and controlling interpretations govern.

## F01

**US Department of Labor, Fact Sheet 23: Overtime Pay Requirements of the FLSA.** Federal general overtime framework for covered, nonexempt employees.

Source: <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/23-flsa-overtime-pay>

Used for the weekly threshold, recurring workweek, non-averaging, and distinction between federal overtime and weekend/holiday arrangements. Does not establish a lineman's exemption status, state coverage, or particular CBA premium.

## F02

**US Department of Labor, Fact Sheet 56A: Overview of the Regular Rate of Pay.** Published December 2019; retrieved on the research date.

Source: <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/56a-regular-rate>

Used for regular-rate inclusions/exclusions and qualified premium credit distinctions. A payment name is not sufficient to determine treatment.

## F03

**US Department of Labor, Fact Sheet 22: Hours Worked Under the FLSA.**

Source: <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/22-flsa-hours-worked>

Used for work-time, breaks, waiting/on-call, and travel distinctions. Individual facts and applicable state rules still require review.

## F04

**US Department of Labor, Fact Sheet 17I: Blue-Collar Workers and the Part 541 exemptions.**

Source: <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/17i-overtime-blue-collar>

Used to reject the assumption that high wages alone make manual linework exempt. This is not an individual duties or worker-classification determination.

## F05

**US Department of Labor, Fact Sheet 56C: Bonuses Under the FLSA.**

Source: <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/56c-bonuses>

Used for nondiscretionary bonuses and allocation to the earnings period. Do not infer discretionary status from the label “bonus.”

## F06

**Electronic Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 778: Overtime Compensation.**

Source: <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-V/subchapter-B/part-778>

Relevant provisions: §§778.104–.105 (workweek); .109 and .115 (regular rate/multiple rates); .201–.207 (premium compensation); .209 (bonuses); .217 (expenses); .218 (certain non-work payments); .220–.222 (reporting/call-back and related payments); .415 onward (conditional alternative methods).

The eCFR is continuously updated; save the actual edition consulted for a shipped rule. A “current” URL does not freeze a historical version. For durable profiles store a content checksum and exact provision, not merely this link.

## F07

**US Department of Labor, Final Rule: Regular Rate Under the FLSA.** 2019 rule explanation.

Source: <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/regular-rate>

Used to avoid carrying outdated call-back exclusions from older guidance into implementation. In particular, the final-rule explanation distinguishes non-prearranged call-back payments from prearranged compensation; do not copy an old “infrequent and sporadic” call-back requirement without checking current regulations.

## F08

**US Department of Labor, Fact Sheet 7: State and Local Governments Under the FLSA.** Revised March 2011; retrieved on the research date.

Source: <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/7-flsa-state-local-government>

Used only to identify public-agency/compensatory-time cases that need a separate rule path. Do not apply police/fire work-period exceptions to a lineworker merely because storm restoration is emergency work.

## S01

**California DLSE, Overtime FAQ.**

Source: <https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/FAQ_Overtime.htm>

Used for California's general daily/weekly/seventh-day framework and the existence of exceptions. Not a complete California utility/construction applicability determination.

## S02

**California Legislature, Labor Code §514.**

Source: <https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=514.>

Used for the conditions of the CBA exception to §§510 and 511. Union membership alone does not satisfy it; the exception is not a waiver of the FLSA.

## S03

**California Department of Industrial Relations, Wage Order 16, 8 CCR §11160.**

Source: <https://www.dir.ca.gov/t8/11160.html>

A construction-industry applicability reference. Do not apply it automatically to every utility employee or assume all states share its provisions.

## P01

**US Department of Labor, Fact Sheet 66E: DBRA Compliance, Fringe Benefit Requirements.**

Source: <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/66E-DBRA-compliance-fringe-benefit-requirements>

Used for separating basic wages and fringe obligations on covered projects. A specific project still needs the incorporated wage determination, classification, dates, and applicable federal/state rules.

## T01

**Internal Revenue Service, Publication 463 (2025), Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses, chapter 6.** Retrieved September 6, 2026; the displayed edition is for 2025 returns, not a 2026 mileage-rate table.

Source: <https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463>

Used for accountable/nonaccountable reimbursement concepts. Do not import a tax substantiation ceiling or mileage rate as a contractual wage promise. Do not infer a payment's regular-rate treatment solely from its tax treatment.

## C01

**California Outside Line Construction Agreement, Western Line Constructors Chapter of NECA and IBEW Locals 47 and 1245.** Stated term: June 1, 2022 to May 31, 2027.

Source: <https://ibew1245.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/California-Outside-Line-Construction-Agreement-2022-2027-signed.pdf>

Scoped findings from the signed document: §4.6 addresses a four-hour unscheduled callout minimum and regular-shift overlap; §4.10 provides specified double-time conditions; §4.13 has conditional rest provisions. Exhibit A's June 1, 2026 journeyman lineman column lists $74.43 wages and $14.33 NEAP separately. These are not a US-wide rate or one combined cash wage. The wage-table page was visually checked during research.

Useful locators: printed pp.18–19 for §§4.6–4.10; printed p.21 for §4.13; printed p.22 for subsistence; printed p.35 for Exhibit A (PDF physical pages 21–22, 24, 25, 38 respectively). Verify page labels against the downloaded edition.

Current project assent, amendments, side letters, worker classification, and territorial applicability were **not** individually established. No complete CBA pack is certified by this handbook. Existing repository [source notes](../../research/california-outside-line-2022-2027.md) contain earlier fixture context; they are not a release certificate.

## Admitting a rule into a released agreement profile

This is a **PRODUCT approval protocol**, not a legal source. Each rule/profile needs a record with:

| Field | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| Identity and scope | Employer/assent, project if relevant, classification, jurisdiction, agreement and amendments |
| Temporal scope | Agreement term, wage effective dates, source retrieval date, applicable workday/workweek/timezone |
| Evidence | Primary source URL, downloaded edition/checksum, exact section/page, approved interpretation |
| Computational contract | Inputs, conditions, exclusions, precedence/stacking, units, money/time rounding, relevant adjacent rules |
| Review | Named responsible human/domain reviewer, review date, unresolved questions, and review status; an AI must not fabricate sign-off |
| Proof | Positive, negative, boundary, interaction, and historical-reproduction fixtures; candidate commit and test evidence |
| Published coverage | Supported clause variants, omissions, unsupported circumstances, and corresponding user-visible warnings |

Promotion states: `candidate → applicability reviewed → semantics reviewed → fixtures approved → implementation verified → released`. A source URL, worker confirmation, passing unit test, and legal/domain review are distinct evidence. None substitutes for the others.

For updates, recheck amendments and effective dates, create a new version, assess affected prior periods without rewriting them, and record what changed. Stop promotion when applicability or a financially material interaction remains unresolved. Narrowly scoped configured calculations can remain useful, but their limitation must be visible.
